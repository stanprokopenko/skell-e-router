# Jev tool-tip tracker prototype, brief for a new lead

Developer brief. Written 2026-11-04 by the lead who ran the Jev pencil-tip box test, for a fresh lead who starts cold. Everything marked verified was checked on Stan's PC today.

## Stan's ask, in his words (Slack, 2026-11-04)

> jev images is proving to be very reliable.. Lets get a new lead to create a new prototype that asks jev a series of questions:
>
> 1. is there a hand holding a tool in the image. If no, then skip that frame. If yes, continue to next step
> 2. Jev puts box around the tip of the tool being held by the hand. The tip is the part that would be used to make marks on the surface (this is important when holding something large and blunt like an eraser or shamy and doesn't have a clear tip). Not the whole tool (I noticed in our samples Jev frequently put the box around the whole pencil not just the tip). Also ask if the tool tip is touching the surface.
> 3. Crop the image at the box + some safety margin since it's not always correct. Send that cropped image with the same question as #2 to see if Jev refines it further.
>
> We do this as a variable amount of frame increments (such as every 4 frames) and this should give us good data on the activity state of the artist's tool through the video.
>
> We run that data through some fancy math to smooth out and make that motion curve more accurate. Things like removing impossible outliers and finding the most likely curves that fit in the noisy position points. Every point has a margin of error, but when looking at a bunch in time, it can inform us of the actual position. You're smart, you can identify the ideal math for this, or try various methods and show me so I can check with my eyes.
>
> Start by doing this to a minute of footage from our test video (the one that I recorded specifically for pencil tip module using various tools) and render that test clip with overlays of the found data so I can watch it in motion.
>
> Oh and we need to keep track of speed and cost of this so we can estimate for longer projects.

It is a prototype. It must not touch the Solar Sailer app, its installer or its deploy.

## What is already known (verified)

- **Jev box accuracy on this footage.** On 23 frames with Stan's hand-placed tip points, Jev 1.14's box center landed a median 121 px from the point on the 3840 by 2160 source (about 32 px on the 1024-wide image Jev saw); 43% of points fell inside the box and 20 of 23 within 35 px of the box edge. Boxes are loose (median 154 by 108 source px) and three of 23 traced the pencil shaft instead of the tip. The wording "The tip of the pencil in `frame`, where the graphite point meets or nearly meets the paper." beat "The pencil tip in `frame`." on every measure, and its confidence is informative: 0.6 or higher gave a 70 px median; the worst miss came at 0.08. Jev always returns a box even when no tip is visible, but confidence drops (median 0.25 on no-tip frames against 0.76 with a tip). Full numbers: [2026-11-04-results.md](../jev-images/2026-11-04-results.md#pencil-tip-box-added-later-on-2026-11-04), per-frame data in `docs/jev-images/2026-11-04-box-results.json`, runner `docs/jev-images/run_box.py`, scorer `docs/jev-images/score_box.py`.

The installed package in the default Python (3.11, `pip show skell-e-router`) is 3.43.0 and matches the checkout, so Solar Sailer's Python sees box support. ## What to build

Put the prototype in solar-sailer under `benchmarks/jev-tool-tip-tracker/` (next to `pencil-contact-probe/` and `jev-chapter-split-probe/`), following that repo's `docs/concurrent-agent-operations.md` for worktree slots and git safety. Nothing under the app's source tree.

1. **Pick one minute.** Default: a window that crosses a tool change and includes a tool-down moment, for example 13:25 to 14:25 (Mono Zero eraser into charcoal). Confirm against the segment map, state the window and the tools in it in the thread, and let Stan redirect. Extract frames every N source frames (default N = 4, so 360 samples per minute at 24 fps; make N a flag) at 1024 wide, keeping the source frame number per sample.
2. **Pipeline per sample, all through `classify("jev-latest", ...)`.**
   - Q1 (noul): is a hand holding a drawing tool in `frame`? Say what counts: pencil, charcoal, eraser of any shape, chamois, stump, finger used to smudge. Skip the frame below a threshold you choose and record the probability either way.
   - Q2 (box plus noul, one request): the tip of the held tool, defined as the part that makes or removes marks on the paper, and explicitly not the whole tool; for a blunt tool like an eraser or chamois, the contact face. Plus "is that tip touching the paper?". Start from the wording that won the box test and add Stan's definition. Record confidence for both.
   - Q3 (refine): crop the 4K source around the Q2 box with a safety margin (start with 2 to 3 times the box size, at least 512 px wide after cropping, since Jev rejects narrower images; upscale if needed), ask Q2 again on the crop, map the answer back to full-frame coordinates. Keep both the raw and the refined box so the overlay can show whether refinement helps.
   Store one JSON line per sample with frame number, timestamp, every answer, confidence, tokens, latency and text-rate cost. Resumable, no key, no image bytes.
3. **Smoothing.** Produce a per-sample tip point (box center, or the refined center) with a confidence, then a smoothed track. Try at least two of: Hampel or median-filter outlier rejection followed by a Savitzky-Golay filter; a constant-velocity Kalman filter with RTS smoothing, measurement noise scaled by Jev's confidence; LOWESS or a robust smoothing spline. Reject physically impossible jumps (a tip cannot move more than a few hundred source px between samples 1/6 s apart while drawing; derive the bound from the data, not a guess). For the touching state, smooth the per-sample probability with hysteresis or a two-state HMM so it does not flicker. Show the variants side by side, or as switchable overlays, so Stan can judge by eye; recommend one.
4. **Render.** A 1080p 24 fps mp4 of the chosen minute (under 50 MB so it can be attached; use a lower bitrate or 720p if needed) with: the raw Jev box, the refined box, the smoothed tip trail, a touching/not-touching badge, the Q1 result on skipped frames, and the running timestamp. Also one PNG strip of the raw versus smoothed x and y curves over time, confidence on a third axis.
5. **Speed and cost ledger (Stan's explicit ask).** Record per request: tokens, latency, text-rate cost. Report per minute of footage: requests, total tokens, wall-clock, and cost at the text rate, with the unconfirmed-image-price caveat. Extrapolate to the full 39-minute take and to a one-hour recording, at N = 4 and at N = 2 and N = 8, sequential and with modest concurrency (TypeSafe's rate limits are not documented; probe gently). Order of magnitude expected: about 1,000 requests, 2 million tokens, $0.10 at the text rate, 3 minutes sequential, for one minute of footage at N = 4.
6. **Write-up.** A developer results doc in the benchmark folder and a short plain-language summary for Stan in the thread (and in the Aingel vault only if he asks). Lead with what he can see in the video.
