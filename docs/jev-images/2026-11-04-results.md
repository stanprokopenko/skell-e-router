# Jev image evaluations, scored results

Developer write-up. Stan's summary is in the Aingel vault at `reports/2026-11-04 Jev image test.md`. Governing plan: `aingel/docs/plans/2026-11-02-jev-image-classification-test.md` with Stan's 2026-11-04 amendments. Checkpoint before this run: [2026-11-04-first-run.md](2026-11-04-first-run.md). Stan reviewed every item on the generated review page (`build_review.py`) and corrected three labels; this version of the write-up includes those corrections and a reworded hand question he asked for.

## Verdict

Jev handles images well enough to be a candidate for both jobs tested. With the reworded hand question, Jev 1.13 gets 51 of 52 frames right, ahead of Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite's 48. On Solar Sailer's seven-clip import benchmark Jev scores 7/7 after Stan corrected one label, against Gemini's 6/7. On drawing stage and pencil contact the two are within one or two items of each other. Jev answers in about 0.14 seconds per frame against Gemini's 1.4 seconds, and at TypeSafe's published text rate it costs about a quarter of Gemini per frame. Its confidence is honest in a way Gemini's is not: on pencil contact, Jev's high-confidence answers were right 94 to 100 percent of the time, while Gemini claimed 0.8 or higher confidence on every frame and was right 80 percent of the time.

Two hard limits. Jev rejects images narrower than 512 pixels with "Invalid image dimensions", so Solar Sailer's production 384-pixel frames cannot be sent as is. And TypeSafe still publishes no image price; every Jev cost below is the text rate applied to the reported input tokens, which is a bound, not a verified bill.

## What ran

All scored calls went through this checkout's `classify()` (Jev) and `ask_ai()` (Gemini). Two Jev versions ran: `jev` resolves to 1.13.0 and `jev-latest` to 1.14.0. The Gemini baseline is `gemini-3.1-flash-lite`, the model Solar Sailer's import step uses today.

Drawing-camera frames came from `D:/solar-sailer/labeling/` and were resized to 1024 pixels on the long side. `build_manifest.py` selected them deterministically (seed 20261104): 50 pencil-labeled frames from the one labeled clip, 8 hand-task frames from four other instructors, 12 drawing-stage frames (first, median and last extracted frame of three clips, plus real start, middle and end of the one clip on disk via ffmpeg), and five best-still groups of four frames at least 300 source frames apart. An Opus subagent labeled hand obstruction, drawing stage and best still in one pass before any model ran, and extended hand obstruction to all 52 distinct frames when the first 20 came out 18 yes to 2 no. Pencil contact uses Stan's own labels. Files: `2026-11-04-manifest.json`, `2026-11-04-labels.json`, and Stan's overrides in `2026-11-04-stan-corrections.json` (H-11 covered becomes yes, S-12 becomes first marks with in progress accepted, I-5 becomes ANI; the S-12 value is the answer all three models gave and still needs Stan's explicit confirmation).

The import benchmark videos were found on the R: share under the Feefal sketchbook-tour episode folder; `run_import.py` records the paths and sizes. Frames were sampled exactly as production does: ten timestamps at duration times (i + 0.5) / 10, width capped with `scale='min(W,iw)':-2`. Gemini received the production prompt verbatim (SHA-256 recorded per row) at both 384 and 512 wide. Jev received the same frames at 512 wide with a Choice question over the twelve codes, plus framing and best-thumbnail questions. Raw rows: `2026-11-04-drawing-runs.jsonl`, `2026-11-04-drawing-gemini-runs.jsonl`, `2026-11-04-drawing-runs-hand-v2.jsonl`, `2026-11-04-drawing-gemini-runs-hand-v2.jsonl`, `2026-11-04-import-runs.jsonl` (384), `2026-11-04-import-runs-512.jsonl`. Scored numbers: `2026-11-04-results.json` from `score.py`. Review IDs quoted below map to frames in `2026-11-04-review-ids.json`.

Hand obstruction. Question 1 was "Does a hand, arm or tool cover part of the drawing on the page in `frame`?" Every model got all "no" frames right and missed only "yes" frames. I viewed the frames Jev missed: in each one a hand rests on blank paper away from the drawn marks. Stan's reading of the review page was the same, Jev was not counting a blank page as the drawing. Question 2 says so explicitly: "Does a hand, arm or tool cover any part of the paper in `frame`? Treat the whole sheet of paper as the drawing, even when it is blank. A hand or tool resting anywhere on the paper counts as covering it; a hand beside the paper or off the page does not." Rerun on all 52 frames, Jev 1.13's misses dropped from 13 to 1, Jev 1.14's from 10 to 3, Gemini's from 10 to 4. The one frame every model still misses is H-11, where an open pencil case overlaps the page edge and Stan ruled it counts as a tool covering the paper; Jev 1.14 also misses H-33 and H-23, and Gemini misses H-33, H-23 and H-48. Jev 1.13's confidence under question 2 tracks its accuracy: 44 of 45 right when its probability sat 0.8 or more from the midpoint, and all 7 of the rest. Gemini reported 0.8 or more on all 52 and was right on 48.

Drawing stage. With S-12 corrected to first marks, Jev's score answer lands on the labeled level in 11 of 12 frames. Its one miss is the last Eliza frame, scored 2.37 where the label is 3 (finished). Gemini gets 12 of 12.

Best still. All three models made the same single disagreement with the labeler: in the Solovev group (B-2) they chose the more finished frame with a hand over the lower third rather than the hand-free but less finished frame. The labeler had listed the models' choice as acceptable. Jev's confidence on best-still ranged 0.35 to 1.0; Gemini reported 0.8 to 1.0.

Pencil contact. This is the number stroke detection cares about. Scored on the 46 frames Stan labeled touching, not-touching or no-tip (majority-class baseline 50%). All three models caught all five "touching" frames. Jev 1.13's errors were mostly no-tip called not-touching (5 of 23). Gemini's were mostly not-touching called touching (4 of 18), the more harmful direction for stroke detection. Confidence bands tell the sharper story:

| Model | Confidence 0.8 or more | 0.5 to 0.8 | Under 0.5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Jev 1.13 | 16/17 (94%) | 19/26 (73%) | 3/3 |
| Jev 1.14 | 12/12 (100%) | 14/19 (74%) | 11/15 (73%) |
| Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite | 37/46 (80%) | none | none |

On the four frames Stan marked cant-tell, Jev 1.13's confidence was 0.33, 0.46, 0.26 and 0.89; Jev 1.14's was 0.43, 0.62, 0.48 and 0.38; Gemini's was 1.0, 1.0, 1.0 and 0.95. The planned 12-frame subset (11 scorable) came out 9/11 for both Jev versions and 10/11 for Gemini; the full 46 is the better estimate.

Jev's second-place probabilities are informative where it is unsure: GFX is its runner-up on the opening, WRK on the face cam, SCN on the gameplay. Gemini's one miss, the DDR gameplay, comes with 0.95 confidence at both widths and a different wrong code each time.

Width caveat. Jev refused the production 384-pixel frames and 480-pixel frames, and accepted 512 and above; token count scales with pixels (731 tokens at 512 by 288, 1,870 at 1024 by 576). Gemini scored the same at 384 and 512, so the width difference is unlikely to explain the gap, but the inputs are not byte-identical across models. Adopting Jev in the import step would mean raising Solar Sailer's frame width from 384 to at least 512.

Seven clips is too few to claim a real accuracy difference. What the replay does show is that Jev handles this task at roughly 15 times lower cost and 15 times lower latency with the same or better outcome on this set.

## Limits of this evidence

Hand-obstruction, drawing-stage and best-still ground truth came from one Claude Opus rater, then reviewed item by item by Stan, who changed three labels. Question 2 was written after seeing where question 1 failed, so its 98 percent is a result on the same 52 frames the wording was tuned against; a fresh set of frames would be the honest confirmation. The hand set is lopsided (44 yes, 8 no). Twelve stage frames and five best-still groups are small. The import set is seven clips with two placeholder labels. None of the accuracy gaps here, in either direction, is larger than three items. The cost and latency gaps are large and consistent across every request.

## Reproduce

Read `TYPESAFE_API_KEY` and `GEMINI_API_KEY` from the agent vault into the child process. Build frames and manifest with `build_manifest.py --scratch <dir> --output <new manifest>`, label before running, then `run_eval.py` (add `--hand-wording v2 --tasks hand_obstruction` for the reworded question), `run_gemini_drawing.py` (same flags), `run_import.py --extract --width 384` and `--width 512` followed by `--models`, then `score.py` with `--corrections`. Each runner appends JSON lines and skips rows already done, and refuses to write a row containing the key or image bytes. `build_review.py` renders the review page into scratch.
